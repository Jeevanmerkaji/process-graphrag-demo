# Engineering Change Management (ECM)

*Process owner: Head of Change Management. Synthetic example document for demonstration purposes.*

Any modification to a released part, assembly or software version must go through engineering change management. A change is usually triggered by a failed prototype test, a field quality issue, a supplier request or a cost-reduction idea.

The process starts when the requester, typically a design engineer, creates a Change Request in the PLM system. The request must describe the affected parts, the reason for the change and a first estimate of cost and timing impact. In practice, engineers spend a lot of time searching the PLM system for earlier changes to the same or similar parts, because previous decisions and their reasons are only stored as free-text attachments.

Next, the change coordinator performs an impact analysis. The coordinator identifies all affected parts in the Bill of Materials, informs the responsible departments (design, purchasing, production planning, homologation) and collects their assessments in the Impact Assessment form. This step is the main bottleneck: assessments arrive by e-mail in different formats and have to be consolidated manually.

The Change Control Board meets weekly and decides on each change based on the Impact Assessment. The decision and its rationale are recorded in the CCB Decision Log. Board members often ask for a short summary of the change, which the change coordinator prepares by hand before each meeting.

Approved changes require the design engineer to update the CAD Model and the Design FMEA in the CAD system. The change coordinator then issues an Engineering Change Notice to production and suppliers.

Finally, if the change affects a homologation-relevant component, the homologation engineer checks the Engineering Change Notice to decide whether the type approval must be updated. Whether a component is homologation-relevant is currently judged from experience.
